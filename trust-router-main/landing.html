The problem

Three hundred answers.
No way to sort them.

Checking one answer costs about as much as doing the work yourself. So an unsorted pile of AI answers is a pile you can't use.

Trust router doesn't try to make the agents smarter. It looks at how each answer was produced (whether independent checks agree, whether published papers back it, whether it states something testable) and never judges the answer itself.

How it works

Three agents. One plain rule.

01

Propose

An analyst turns your question into four to six specific findings, each one written as a claim you could check.

Analyst · Claude Sonnet

02

Check against papers

A librarian searches Europe PMC and the Pan-Cancer Atlas papers, ranks the passages, and judges each finding against them. A reviewer then asks whether the evidence really holds.

Librarian · Claude Haiku  /  Reviewer · Claude Opus

03

Sort

Plain code, not a model, adds up the checks and sorts each finding into a tier. A check can lower trust but never raise it past what the others allow.

Router · no model

Every verdict shows its reasons: who checked it, what they were shown, and why they concluded what they did. Nothing is hidden behind a score.

What we learned

Independence between agents matters more than the number of agents.

A run with four copies of the same model scored no better than random: the copies make the same mistakes, so their agreement means nothing. The working version paired two different model families.

Same model in every role1.1×
Different model families2.2×

So the live app runs its three agents on three different models.

Honest limits

A tool about trust should say
where not to trust it.

  • The top tier is small. It holds 23 answers, so its true accuracy is roughly 50–89%, not exactly 69.6%.
  • These are zero-shot baselines. Full agentic runs need Docker and hours, and haven't been scored yet.
  • Fresh hypotheses can't be scored. The router can sort them, but nobody knows which are true. Every accuracy figure comes from BixBench.
  • No biologist on the project. The benchmark says which answers are wrong, but not how badly. A slightly wrong answer and a dangerous one look the same.
